---
title: "What belongs in a first version"
date: "2026-08-18"
excerpt: "A first product has one job. Everything else can wait until people have used that job."
tags: ["Product"]
---

A first version is not a smaller copy of the finished company. It is the thinnest thing that lets a real person do one job.

That sounds obvious. It is the part that slips.

Someone describes the product they will have in two years: accounts, roles, reports, a marketplace, a mobile app, an admin, a public site. All of that may be right later. None of it tells you what to build in the next few weeks.

I start with a narrower question: who is the first user, and what do they need to finish on the first day the product exists?

If that answer is fuzzy, the build will be too. If it is sharp — “a founder can collect a waitlist and email those people” — then scope has a floor and a ceiling. Screens that do not serve that sentence wait.

Waiting is not the same as refusing. It is a list you can return to after someone has used v1. Usage is a better editor than a workshop.

I have shipped inside large companies and I have shipped tools I use at home. The pattern is the same. The work that holds up is the work that had a job on day one. The rest was extra until it wasn’t.

If you are sitting on an idea, you do not need a complete map. You need the one job, the first user, and a few weeks to make that real. We can argue about the second version after there is a first.
