---
title: "What belongs in a first version"
date: "2026-08-18"
excerpt: "A first product has one job, and everything else can wait until people have used it."
tags: ["Product"]
---

A first version is the thinnest thing that lets a real person do one job. That sounds obvious, and it's still the part that slips most often.

Someone describes the product they'll have in two years: accounts, roles, reports, a marketplace, a mobile app, an admin panel, a public site. All of that may be right later, but none of it tells you what to build in the next few weeks.

I start with a narrower question. Who is the first user, and what do they need to finish on the first day the product exists?

If that answer is fuzzy, the build will be too. If it's sharp ("a founder can collect a waitlist and email those people"), then scope has a floor and a ceiling, and any screen that doesn't serve that sentence waits.

The screens that wait go on a list you can come back to once someone has used version one. What people do with the product will tell you more about that list than a planning workshop would.

I've shipped inside large companies and I've shipped tools I use at home. In both places, the work that held up had a clear job on day one, and the rest earned its place later or didn't.

If you're sitting on an idea, start with the one job and the first user. A few weeks is usually enough to make that real, and the second version is much easier to argue about once a first one exists.
